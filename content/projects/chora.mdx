## Summary

Chora puts the small things you reach for all day into the space at the top of
your Mac. A master volume slider and one for each app, your day as a timeline
you can edit, a timer, and your music with album art, a live visualizer, and
synced lyrics. I started from the open-source boring.notch and built the parts
I wanted from my own notch. It's free to download, and updates install
themselves.

![The notch expanded: a master volume slider and a separate slider for each app](/media/chora/volume.png)

## The problem

The notch is prime real estate that most apps ignore, and the controls I use
constantly are scattered. Turning down one app means turning down everything.
Checking my next event means opening a calendar. A timer means another app.
Each one is small, but together they break focus dozens of times a day.

## Target user

People who live on a MacBook with a notch and want small, frequent actions one
glance away: students, designers, and developers who juggle music, calls,
meetings, and focus sessions at once.

## Competitors

- **Notch utilities like NotchNook**: cover media controls and shortcuts, but
  not per-app volume or a calendar you can edit.
- **macOS itself**: Control Center has one volume slider for the whole system,
  and no timeline you can drag around.

Chora's bet: the notch should be a place to do things, not just look at them.

## Insight

Four design principles guide it:

- **One glance, one action.** Everything fits in a short, wide space, so each
  tab does one thing and does it fast.
- **Control what you'd otherwise open an app for.** Per-app volume, dragging an
  event, undoing a reset timer.
- **Sharing space gracefully.** Music and a running timer sit in the notch
  together instead of fighting for it.
- **Stay out of the way.** It should be reliable enough to forget about, and
  updates should install without you thinking about them.

## Solution

### Volume, per app

A master slider plus a slider for each app. Turn Discord down without touching
Spotify.

### A calendar you can edit

Your day is one wide timeline. Drag events to move or resize them, click for
details, and see your Google Tasks alongside.

![Your day as a timeline in the notch, with events you can drag to move or resize](/media/chora/calendar.png)

### A timer that forgives mistakes

Lengths down to the second, a list of recent timers, a smooth progress ring,
and undo when you reset by accident.

![The timer tab](/media/chora/timer.png)

### Music that shares the space

Album art, a live visualizer, and synced lyrics. When a timer is running, it
sits next to the music instead of replacing it.

![The music tab with album art and a live visualizer](/media/chora/music.png)

### Quick to move around

Switch tabs with ⌥1 to ⌥5. A shelf holds files you drop on the notch, and
clipboard history gets back anything you copied.

## Distribution

Chora is a free download as a .dmg for Apple silicon Macs on macOS 14 or later.
macOS blocks apps it hasn't verified, so the first run needs a one-line
Terminal command. The site puts the install steps right under the download
button so nobody gets stuck. After that, signed updates install from inside the
app. There's a Discord for setups and early versions, and a feedback form for
bugs and requests.

## Adapting to user feedback

The site has a public "Coming soon" list: switching your music to this Mac,
connecting Google Calendar directly for faster updates, and finishing the Chora
name and icon inside the app. A "Your idea" box invites requests that go
straight onto that list. I use Chora every day, and the first things I built
were the ones I kept missing.
