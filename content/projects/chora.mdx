## Summary

Chora puts the small things you reach for all day into the space at the top of
your Mac: a master volume slider and one for each app, your day as a calendar
you can edit, a timer, and your music with album art, a live visualizer, and
synced lyrics. I lead it end to end: I decide what belongs in the notch, design
each tab, build it in Swift, and run the releases. It started from the
open-source boring.notch and has become its own product, with its own name,
website, roadmap, and update channel. It's free, and updates install
themselves.

![The notch expanded: a master volume slider and a separate slider for each app](/media/chora/volume.png)

## The problem

The notch is the most visible space on a MacBook, and most apps ignore it.
Meanwhile the controls I use constantly are scattered. Turning down one app
means turning down everything. Checking my next event means opening a
calendar. A timer means another app. Each one is small, but together they
break focus dozens of times a day.

## Target user

People who live on a MacBook with a notch and want small, frequent actions one
glance away: students, designers, and developers who juggle music, calls,
meetings, and focus sessions at once.

## Competitors

- **Notch utilities like NotchNook**: cover media controls and shortcuts, but
  not per-app volume or a calendar you can edit.
- **macOS itself**: Control Center has one volume slider for the whole system,
  and no timeline you can drag around.

Chora's bet: the notch should be a place to do things, not just look at them.

## Insight

Four design principles decide what gets built and what gets cut:

- **One glance, one action.** Everything fits in a short, wide space, so each
  tab does one thing and does it fast. If it needs a full window, it doesn't
  belong in the notch.
- **Control what you'd otherwise open an app for.** Per-app volume, dragging an
  event, undoing a reset timer.
- **Sharing space gracefully.** Music and a running timer sit in the notch
  together instead of fighting for it.
- **Stay out of the way.** It should be reliable enough to forget about, and
  updates should install without you thinking about them.

## Solution

### Volume, per app

A master slider plus a slider for each app. Turn Discord down without touching
Spotify.

### A calendar you can edit

Your day as one wide timeline, or top to bottom like a phone calendar. Drag
events to move them, pull an edge to change the time, click for details, undo
any change, and see your Google Tasks alongside.

![Your day as a timeline in the notch, with events you can drag to move or resize](/media/chora/calendar.png)

### A timer that forgives mistakes

Lengths down to the second, a list of recent timers, a smooth progress ring,
and undo when you reset by accident.

![The timer tab](/media/chora/timer.png)

### Music that shares the space

Album art, a live visualizer, and synced lyrics, now on by default. When a
timer is running, it sits next to the music instead of replacing it.

![The music tab with album art and a live visualizer](/media/chora/music.png)

### Quick to move around

Switch tabs with ⌥1 to ⌥5. A shelf holds files you drop on the notch, and
clipboard history gets back anything you copied.

## Design decisions

### Two shapes for the same day

The notch is wide and short, so the calendar started as a horizontal timeline:
the whole day at a glance. But people read their day top to bottom, the way
phone and Google calendars show it, and a wide strip makes long days and
overlapping events hard to scan. Rather than pick one, the latest release adds
a top-to-bottom view behind a single button. Overlapping events sit side by
side, and the top and bottom edges of an event change its time.

### Letting people choose the size

A top-to-bottom day needs height the notch doesn't have. Instead of a fixed
"big" size, you drag the notch's bottom edge to the height you want. The expand
button then becomes a shrink button, and expanding again returns to the size
you last chose, so the notch remembers your preference instead of making you
redo it.

### Undo instead of dialogs

Dragging or deleting an event in a space this small invites mistakes, but a
confirmation dialog on every move would slow down every change to guard
against a rare one. So moves and resizes happen straight away, delete only
asks for a second tap on the same button, and every change can be undone, one
step at a time.

## Distribution

Chora is a free download as a .dmg for Apple silicon Macs on macOS 14 or later.
macOS blocks apps it hasn't verified, so the first run needs a one-line
Terminal command. The site puts the install steps right under the download
button so nobody gets stuck. After that, signed updates install from inside the
app. There's a Discord for setups and early versions, and a feedback form for
bugs and requests.

## Adapting to user feedback

The site has a public "Coming soon" list, and a "Your idea" box sends requests
straight onto it. The feedback form asks for what I need to act on a report:
steps to reproduce, the app and macOS version, and what you expected. I use
Chora every day, and the first things I built were the ones I kept missing.

## Process

I run Chora like a small product team of one. Every change starts as a written
plan, gets tried on my own Mac, and ships only after it works. Releases are
small and frequent: 19 versions since September 2026, each with a
plain-language changelog entry that becomes the in-app update note, the GitHub
release, and the website's "What's new" at once. One release script builds,
signs, and publishes each version, so shipping a fix takes minutes and reaches
everyone through the in-app updater.
